Long Beach 2017: "Attention Is All You Need"

In which Heqing Kwek Lee solves the multi-bowl cafeteria problem, Google abolishes sequential recurrence with parallel bilinear metric tensors, and complex rotary geometry (RoPE) preserves Euclidean distance across sequence time.

"In December 2017, the neural network community descended upon the Long Beach Convention Center under the warm southern California sun. The atmosphere was charged with a palpable tension. For four years, Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTMs and GRUs) had reigned supreme over natural language processing. But behind closed doors, every systems engineer and machine learning researcher was in agony. Modern NVIDIA GPUs were massive parallel vector processors boasting thousands of SIMD floating-point cores. Yet, to process a sequence of 512 tokens with an LSTM, you were forced to dispatch 512 sequential CUDA kernel launches, chaining hidden states \(\mathbf{h}_t = f(\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t)\) in an excruciating serial queue.

Then came paper #7181 from Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan Gomez, Łukasz Kaiser, and Illia Polosukhin. Its title was an audacious manifesto: 'Attention Is All You Need'. The authors didn't merely propose a new gate or cell variant. They committed architectural heresy: they discarded recurrence completely. In one stroke, sequence modeling was unrolled into a single, colossal \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) parallel matrix multiplication kernel."

— From the Memoirs of a Veteran AI Scholar

3.1 The Geometry of Attention: Bilinear Forms as Metric Kernels

Let an input sequence of length \(N\) embedded in dimension \(d_{\text{model}}\) be represented as a matrix:

\[ \mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_{\text{model}}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x}_1^\top \\ \mathbf{x}_2^\top \\ \vdots \\ \mathbf{x}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \]

In classical metric geometry, to evaluate the relationship between two vectors \(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}\), one might evaluate a standard Euclidean dot product \(\mathbf{x}_i^\top \mathbf{x}_j\). However, this is symmetric (\(\mathbf{x}_i^\top \mathbf{x}_j = \mathbf{x}_j^\top \mathbf{x}_i\)) and treats all feature dimensions identically. In natural language and reasoning, interaction is inherently asymmetric and context-dependent: a verb looking for its direct object performs a fundamentally different interrogation than an object seeking its governing verb.

To achieve this flexibility, we define three learnable linear projection operators:

\[ \mathbf{W}_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}, \quad \mathbf{W}_K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}, \quad \mathbf{W}_V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_v} \]

Transforming our inputs into Queries, Keys, and Values:

\[ \mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_Q \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_k}, \quad \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_K \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_k}, \quad \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{X}\mathbf{W}_V \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_v} \]
Linear Algebra Insight: The General Bilinear Form

Notice what happens when we compute the inner product between query vector \(\mathbf{q}_i = \mathbf{W}_Q^\top \mathbf{x}_i\) and key vector \(\mathbf{k}_j = \mathbf{W}_K^\top \mathbf{x}_j\):

\[ \langle \mathbf{q}_i, \mathbf{k}_j \rangle = \mathbf{q}_i^\top \mathbf{k}_j = (\mathbf{x}_i^\top \mathbf{W}_Q) (\mathbf{W}_K^\top \mathbf{x}_j) = \mathbf{x}_i^\top (\mathbf{W}_Q \mathbf{W}_K^\top) \mathbf{x}_j \]

Let \(\mathbf{M} \equiv \mathbf{W}_Q \mathbf{W}_K^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{model}}}\). The unscaled attention score is a bilinear metric form parameterized by matrix \(\mathbf{M}\):

\[ B(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j) = \mathbf{x}_i^\top \mathbf{M} \mathbf{x}_j \]

Because \(\mathbf{W}_Q\) and \(\mathbf{W}_K\) are distinct, independent matrices, \(\mathbf{M}\) is non-symmetric: \(\mathbf{M} \neq \mathbf{M}^\top\). This asymmetry allows \(\mathbf{x}_i\) to strongly attend to \(\mathbf{x}_j\) without requiring \(\mathbf{x}_j\) to attend back to \(\mathbf{x}_i\) with the same intensity. Furthermore, because \(d_k < d_{\text{model}}\), \(\mathbf{M}\) has rank at most \(d_k\), inducing a low-rank bottleneck that extracts principal relational modes.

3.2 Softmax Temperature & The Variance Scaling Proof (\(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}\))

The full Scaled Dot-Product Attention operator is formulated as:

\[ \text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} \right) \mathbf{V} \]

Why the scaling factor \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}\)? Why not \(\frac{1}{d_k}\) or simply \(1\)? At the Long Beach poster session in 2017, many attendees assumed this was an empirical heuristic. It is not. It is a strict necessity derived from the variance of high-dimensional inner products under the central limit theorem.

Rigorous Probability Proof: Dot-Product Variance Expansion

Assumptions: Let the components of query vector \(\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}\) and key vector \(\mathbf{k} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k}\) be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with zero mean and unit variance:

\[ \mathbb{E}[q_l] = \mathbb{E}[k_l] = 0, \quad \operatorname{Var}(q_l) = \operatorname{Var}(k_l) = 1, \quad \forall l \in \{1, \dots, d_k\} \]

Consider the scalar dot product \(S = \mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{k} = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} q_l k_l\).

Step 1: Expected Value of the Product: By statistical independence of \(\mathbf{q}\) and \(\mathbf{k}\): \[ \mathbb{E}[q_l k_l] = \mathbb{E}[q_l] \mathbb{E}[k_l] = 0 \cdot 0 = 0 \] Therefore, by linearity of expectation: \[ \mathbb{E}[S] = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} \mathbb{E}[q_l k_l] = 0 \]

Step 2: Variance of Individual Terms: For any two independent random variables \(X, Y\) with zero mean: \[ \operatorname{Var}(XY) = \mathbb{E}[(XY)^2] - (\mathbb{E}[XY])^2 = \mathbb{E}[X^2 Y^2] - 0 = \mathbb{E}[X^2] \mathbb{E}[Y^2] \] Since \(\mathbb{E}[q_l^2] = \operatorname{Var}(q_l) = 1\) and \(\mathbb{E}[k_l^2] = \operatorname{Var}(k_l) = 1\): \[ \operatorname{Var}(q_l k_l) = 1 \cdot 1 = 1 \]

Step 3: Variance of the Sum: Because the dimensions \(l \in \{1, \dots, d_k\}\) are mutually independent, the variance of the sum is the sum of the variances: \[ \operatorname{Var}(S) = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} \operatorname{Var}(q_l k_l) = \sum_{l=1}^{d_k} 1 = d_k \] \[ \sigma_S = \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(S)} = \sqrt{d_k} \]

The Consequence on Softmax Gradients:
In modern architectures, \(d_k = 128\). Without scaling, the standard deviation of raw logit scores is \(\sqrt{128} \approx 11.31\). When logits differ by values on the order of 10 to 20, the \(\text{Softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}\) distribution collapses into a one-hot indicator: \[ \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{z}) \to [0, \dots, 0, 1, 0, \dots, 0]^\top \] Recall the Jacobian of the Softmax function: \[ \frac{\partial \text{Softmax}(\mathbf{z})_i}{\partial z_j} = s_i (\delta_{ij} - s_j) \] When \(s_i \to 1\) and all other \(s_j \to 0\), the derivative vanishes everywhere: \[ s_i(1 - s_i) \to 0, \quad -s_i s_j \to 0 \] The gradients vanish completely, stopping all backpropagation! Dividing by \(\sqrt{d_k}\) scales the variance back to \(\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{S}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) = \frac{d_k}{d_k} = 1\), holding the Softmax comfortably in its maximum entropy, non-saturated gradient regime.

3.3 Position Encodings: From Absolute Sinusoids to Rotary Embeddings (RoPE)

Standard matrix operations in self-attention are permutation equivariant:

\[ \text{Attention}(\mathbf{P}\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{P}\mathbf{K}, \mathbf{P}\mathbf{V}) = \mathbf{P} \, \text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) \]

for any permutation matrix \(\mathbf{P}\). To model sequence grammar, positional coordinates must be injected. While the original 2017 paper added static absolute sinusoidal vectors to \(\mathbf{X}\), modern foundation models (Llama 3, DeepSeek-R1, Qwen 2.5, Mistral) employ Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) (Su et al., 2021).

The Core Objective of Relative Encoding

We desire a transformation \(\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{x}, m)\) that takes a vector \(\mathbf{x}\) at index \(m\) such that the inner product between query at position \(m\) and key at position \(n\) depends purely on the relative distance \(n - m\):

\[ \langle \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{q}, m), \mathbf{R}(\mathbf{k}, n) \rangle = g(\mathbf{q}, \mathbf{k}, n - m) \]
Complex Geometric Derivation: The 2D Rotation Manifold

Let us represent a 2-dimensional vector \(\mathbf{q} = [q_1, q_2]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2\) as a complex number: \[ z_q = q_1 + i q_2 \in \mathbb{C} \] To encode integer position \(m\), we multiply by a unit complex phasor with angular frequency \(\theta\): \[ \tilde{z}_q = z_q \cdot e^{i m \theta} = (q_1 + i q_2)(\cos m\theta + i \sin m\theta) \]

Expanding into real and imaginary components: \[ \operatorname{Re}(\tilde{z}_q) = q_1 \cos m\theta - q_2 \sin m\theta \] \[ \operatorname{Im}(\tilde{z}_q) = q_1 \sin m\theta + q_2 \cos m\theta \]

Expressing this linear map as a \(2 \times 2\) orthogonal rotation matrix \(\mathbf{R}_{\theta, m} \in \text{SO}(2)\):

\[ \mathbf{R}_{\theta, m} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos m\theta & -\sin m\theta \\ \sin m\theta & \cos m\theta \end{bmatrix} \]

Now, observe what happens to the inner product between rotated query \(\tilde{\mathbf{q}}_m = \mathbf{R}_{\theta, m} \mathbf{q}\) and rotated key \(\tilde{\mathbf{k}}_n = \mathbf{R}_{\theta, n} \mathbf{k}\):

\[ \langle \tilde{\mathbf{q}}_m, \tilde{\mathbf{k}}_n \rangle = (\mathbf{R}_{\theta, m} \mathbf{q})^\top (\mathbf{R}_{\theta, n} \mathbf{k}) = \mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{R}_{\theta, m}^\top \mathbf{R}_{\theta, n} \mathbf{k} \]

Because 2D rotation matrices commute and \(\mathbf{R}_{\theta, m}^\top = \mathbf{R}_{\theta, -m}\):

\[ \mathbf{R}_{\theta, m}^\top \mathbf{R}_{\theta, n} = \mathbf{R}_{\theta, -m} \mathbf{R}_{\theta, n} = \mathbf{R}_{\theta, n - m} \]
\[ \mathbf{q}^\top \mathbf{R}_{\theta, n - m} \mathbf{k} = \mathbf{q}^\top \begin{bmatrix} \cos (n-m)\theta & -\sin (n-m)\theta \\ \sin (n-m)\theta & \cos (n-m)\theta \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{k} \]

The Mathematical Triumph: The dot product depends strictly on the relative displacement \(\Delta = n - m\)! If a query at position 100 looks at a key at position 105, the interaction angle is identical to a query at position 1 looking at a key at position 6.

Extension to Dimension \(d\): Block-Diagonal Orthogonal Group \(\text{SO}(d)\)

For a vector space of dimension \(d\) (where \(d\) is even), we decompose \(\mathbb{R}^d\) into \(\frac{d}{2}\) orthogonal 2D subspaces. Each 2D plane rotates at a geometrically decaying base frequency:

\[ \theta_i = b^{-2(i-1)/d}, \quad \text{for } i \in \left\{1, 2, \dots, \frac{d}{2}\right\}, \quad b = 10000 \]

The global rotation operator \(\mathbf{R}_{\Theta, m} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}\) is a block-diagonal matrix:

\[ \mathbf{R}_{\Theta, m} = \operatorname{diag}\left( \mathbf{R}_{\theta_1, m}, \mathbf{R}_{\theta_2, m}, \dots, \mathbf{R}_{\theta_{d/2}, m} \right) \]

In hardware implementations, constructing explicit \(d \times d\) block matrices is wasteful. Instead, we compute the rotation element-wise using Hadamard products and a sign-flipped half-vector permutation:

\[ \mathbf{R}_{\Theta, m} \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x} \odot \cos(m \boldsymbol{\Theta}) + \operatorname{rotate\_half}(\mathbf{x}) \odot \sin(m \boldsymbol{\Theta}) \]

3.4 Multi-Head Attention (MHA): Subspace Specialization

Instead of performing a single attention function with \(d_{\text{model}}\)-dimensional keys, queries, and values, we project the queries, keys, and values \(h\) times with different learned linear projections:

\[ \text{MultiHead}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \operatorname{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h) \mathbf{W}_O \] \[ \text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{W}_i^Q, \mathbf{K}\mathbf{W}_i^K, \mathbf{V}\mathbf{W}_i^V) \]

where \(\mathbf{W}_i^Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}\), \(\mathbf{W}_i^K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}\), \(\mathbf{W}_i^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_v}\), and \(\mathbf{W}_O \in \mathbb{R}^{h d_v \times d_{\text{model}}}\).

Typically, we set \(d_k = d_v = d_{\text{model}} / h\). This ensures that the total computational cost of multi-head attention is identical to single-head attention with full dimensionality, while allowing each head to specialize in distinct geometric subspaces (syntactic dependency, entity tracking, coreference resolution).

3.5 Ground-Zero PyTorch Implementation: Causal Multi-Head Attention with RoPE

Below is the complete, self-contained, batched implementation. It includes the exact RoPE complex frequency caching and causal autoregressive masking: 

import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
import math

class RotaryEmbedding(nn.Module):
    """
    Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) as defined in Su et al. (2021).
    Encodes relative position by rotating queries and keys in 2D coordinate subspaces.
    """
    def __init__(self, dim: int, max_seq_len: int = 4096, base: float = 10000.0):
        super().__init__()
        self.dim = dim
        self.max_seq_len = max_seq_len
        self.base = base
        
        # Calculate angular frequencies: theta_i = base^(-2(i-1)/dim)
        inv_freq = 1.0 / (base ** (torch.arange(0, dim, 2).float() / dim))
        self.register_buffer("inv_freq", inv_freq)
        self._build_cache(max_seq_len)

    def _build_cache(self, seq_len: int):
        # Position indices: [0, 1, ..., seq_len - 1]
        t = torch.arange(seq_len, dtype=torch.float32)
        
        # Outer product: [seq_len, dim / 2]
        freqs = torch.outer(t, self.inv_freq)
        
        # Duplicate frequencies to match full head_dim: [seq_len, dim]
        emb = torch.cat((freqs, freqs), dim=-1)
        
        # Cache cos and sin tensors shaped for broadcasting: (1, 1, seq_len, dim)
        self.register_buffer("cos_cached", emb.cos().unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0), persistent=False)
        self.register_buffer("sin_cached", emb.sin().unsqueeze(0).unsqueeze(0), persistent=False)

    def _rotate_half(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        # Permutes 2D coordinate pairs: [-x2, x1]
        x1 = x[..., : self.dim // 2]
        x2 = x[..., self.dim // 2 :]
        return torch.cat((-x2, x1), dim=-1)

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor, seq_len: int) -> torch.Tensor:
        # x shape: (batch_size, num_heads, seq_len, head_dim)
        if seq_len > self.max_seq_len:
            self._build_cache(seq_len)
            
        cos = self.cos_cached[:, :, :seq_len, :]
        sin = self.sin_cached[:, :, :seq_len, :]
        return (x * cos) + (self._rotate_half(x) * sin)


class CausalMultiHeadAttention(nn.Module):
    """
    Standard Transformer Causal Multi-Head Attention block with RoPE.
    Operates in O(N^2) memory without kernel fusion (pure didactic PyTorch).
    """
    def __init__(self, d_model: int, num_heads: int, max_seq_len: int = 4096):
        super().__init__()
        assert d_model % num_heads == 0, "d_model must be divisible by num_heads"
        
        self.d_model = d_model
        self.num_heads = num_heads
        self.head_dim = d_model // num_heads
        
        # Linear projections for Query, Key, Value, and Output
        self.q_proj = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)
        self.k_proj = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)
        self.v_proj = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)
        self.out_proj = nn.Linear(d_model, d_model, bias=False)
        
        # Rotary Position Embedding operator
        self.rope = RotaryEmbedding(dim=self.head_dim, max_seq_len=max_seq_len)

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        batch_size, seq_len, d_model = x.shape
        
        # 1. Linear Projections: (B, N, D) -> (B, N, num_heads, head_dim) -> (B, num_heads, N, head_dim)
        q = self.q_proj(x).view(batch_size, seq_len, self.num_heads, self.head_dim).transpose(1, 2)
        k = self.k_proj(x).view(batch_size, seq_len, self.num_heads, self.head_dim).transpose(1, 2)
        v = self.v_proj(x).view(batch_size, seq_len, self.num_heads, self.head_dim).transpose(1, 2)
        
        # 2. Inject Rotary Position Embeddings into Queries and Keys
        q = self.rope(q, seq_len)
        k = self.rope(k, seq_len)
        
        # 3. Scaled Dot-Product Attention: Q * K^T / sqrt(head_dim)
        scale = 1.0 / math.sqrt(self.head_dim)
        scores = torch.matmul(q, k.transpose(-2, -1)) * scale
        
        # 4. Lower-triangular Causal Autoregressive Mask
        # Future tokens cannot attend to past tokens: M_ij = -inf for j > i
        causal_mask = torch.tril(torch.ones(seq_len, seq_len, device=x.device)).view(1, 1, seq_len, seq_len)
        scores = scores.masked_fill(causal_mask == 0, float("-inf"))
        
        # 5. Attention Weights and Value Aggregation
        attn_weights = F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)
        context = torch.matmul(attn_weights, v) # (B, num_heads, N, head_dim)
        
        # 6. Concat Heads & Final Projection: (B, num_heads, N, head_dim) -> (B, N, D)
        context = context.transpose(1, 2).contiguous().view(batch_size, seq_len, d_model)
        return self.out_proj(context)

    B, N, D = 2, 8, 64  # 2 sequences, 8 tokens, 64 hidden dimensions
    num_heads = 4
    
    layer = CausalMultiHeadAttention(d_model=D, num_heads=num_heads)
    dummy_tokens = torch.randn(B, N, D)
    
    output = layer(dummy_tokens)
    print(f"Input Tensor Shape:  {dummy_tokens.shape}")
    print(f"Output Tensor Shape: {output.shape}")
    assert output.shape == (B, N, D), "Output tensor dimensions must match input!"
